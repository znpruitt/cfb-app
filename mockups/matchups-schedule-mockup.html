Mockup notes (not part of the design)
  • Same component, no fork. Both views consume the scoreboard shipped in Item 87 slices 1–4. Nothing here is a new row shape — the additions are the two contract widenings recorded separately: the prefix slot accepting an FCS marker as well as a rank, and a neutral-site marker on the meta line.
  • Matchups gains a correctness fix, not just a restyle. The shipped row reads Colorado @ Georgia Tech with vs BHooper beneath it, which never says which team belongs to which owner. Team-primary with an owner suffix resolves it structurally — the same defect and the same fix as the Overview redesign.
  • Eyebrows are bronze (#c9a66b), a deliberate deviation from the shipped blue. Bronze was decided earlier in this campaign, ranked above sky, neutral and fuchsia. An intermediate mockup reverted it to the shipped text-blue-300 after a reviewer flagged gold as champion-reserved — that bundled two claims, and only one holds. Shipped is blue is descriptive, and a mockup proposing a change is what deviates from it. Gold is champion-reserved is answered by temporal separation: the champion treatment does not render until a title is awarded — podium cards for #1–#3 are neutral all season — so bronze is uncontested through the year, and at season end the two remain distinguishable, since bronze is a desaturated tan against a dark saturated gold. The reservation binds a token to a purpose, not a hue neighbourhood.
  • Blue would also have been a poor choice independently — it is the interactive token, so eyebrows would share a colour with links and controls.
  • All eyebrows render as pills — one treatment, no per-class variation. An earlier draft gave the outcome tag (Upset) a pill while selection tags (Ranked spotlight, Top matchup) stayed plain, reasoning that they are different classes: pre-game selection reasons versus post-game outcome facts. That distinction is real but undecodable — a reader cannot learn "pill means outcome" from looking, so the shape difference was a distinction the design knew and did not communicate. Rejected. Within each state a tagged row already stands out from untagged rows, so shape does no scanning work; only the word does.
  • Pills rather than plain text. Adding a border cuts against this campaign's direction of removing chrome, but the tags carry more presence with one, and a hairline border on a 10px label is a long way from card chrome. This also recovers some of the weight lost when the amber upset border is deleted.
  • The cost is explicit: this is quieter than the amber border it replaces. A border around a card catches the eye across sixty rows; an eyebrow does not. That is acceptable if Schedule is a reference surface and making games jump out is Featured's and the recap's job — but it is a real reduction, and slice 5 should record it as a decision rather than let the border lapse when the card chrome it lives on is deleted. If upsets should stay prominent, the honest instrument is a hue from INSIGHTS-017-PALETTE, not a shape difference.
  • This is what replaces the amber upset border. The base addendum exempts that border as "emphasis, out of scope for every slice", but the transition deletes the card chrome it lives on — so the exemption would lapse by side effect rather than by decision. The outcome-tag treatment carries the emphasis forward in a form the new row anatomy supports, and slice 5 should record the border as deliberately retired rather than leaving an on-record exemption to expire unnoticed.
  • The card owner's team row carries a neutral background tint. Answers member feedback that the Matchups screen should distinguish an owner's own teams. Background rather than text weight: weight already carries winner/loser on final rows, so emphasising the owner's team that way would render a losing team of theirs bold-and-dimmed — two signals arguing on one row. A tint sits behind the text and leaves the outcome hierarchy alone.
  • Dimming the card owner is dropped. An earlier draft offered it as the alternative treatment, on the reasoning that the repeated name was noise. The tint supersedes it — it distinguishes the row positively rather than by suppressing the other one, and the owner name stays legible. The comparison toggle is removed with it.
  • Neutral rather than owner colour, unless neutral reads too quietly. DESIGN.md reserves owner colour for lists acting as a chart legend, so using it here would be a rule change rather than an application. It would also be a third identity colour on one row alongside the team-colour bar. The toggle keeps an owner-colour variant for comparison only.
  • Residual: the feedback asked for "my teams". On a member's own card the card owner and the member coincide, so this answers it; on another owner's card it highlights their teams. Highlighting the viewing member's teams anywhere requires a user↔owner mapping that does not exist — filed separately.
  • The scoreboard is the default Schedule row — no one-line collapse. The shipped collapse hides the wrong tier. Tier 1 is teams, owners, records and score, which is what a schedule is for; tier 2 is venue, moneyline and conference, which genuinely is secondary. Collapsing to one line hid tier 1 to protect against tier 2. Now the scoreboard always renders and only tier 2 sits behind "More". Scores and owner mapping are visible without interaction, and the row treatment matches Matchups and Overview exactly.
  • The cost is roughly double the scroll — two columns and around 60 games a week puts a full slate near 2,000px. That is acceptable given the filter cuts it to the live handful in one click, and given the alternative was hiding scores behind a click on a results view. Judge the density here against a real slate before accepting it.
  • Broadcast network is tier 1 on a schedule. "Can I watch this" is the question the view answers, so the network sits in the status row beside the kickoff time rather than behind an expand. It renders on scheduled and live rows only — a final's broadcast is dead information, since the row's job at that point is the result. Same principle as the anchor: the status row carries what is actionable for that state.
  • Games without a listed broadcast simply omit it rather than rendering a placeholder; several FCS matchups have none.
  • Conference sits in tier 2 on its own line, not appended to the odds. It is context rather than something a member acts on, and the page already carries a conference filter, so the dimension is handled at page level — the row only needs to state it. Appending it to the odds string made it read as an afterthought and coupled two unrelated facts.
  • Same-conference games collapse. "ACC vs ACC" is nonsense; those render "ACC matchup". Conference is strictly a team attribute, so the "X vs Y" form is a game-level summary of two facts and needs this special case.
  • Alternative if conference proves tier 1: abbreviate it (ACC, B1G, MW) and place it inline on the team line. Rejected for now — that line already carries a colour bar, rank or FCS marker, team, record and owner, and conference would be the sixth element. If the page-level filter makes conference prominent enough that members expect it per row, the inline form is the fallback and something else has to give way.
  • Expansion may not survive. With the network promoted, what remains behind it is venue and city, moneyline, and conference matchup — mildly useful, niche, and inferable respectively. If tier 2 goes unused it can be dropped entirely and the row becomes a plain scoreboard with no interaction. Retained for now because removing information that exists today should be a decision, not an assumption.
  • The status pills become a real filter. They were previously a colour key for the status-coloured cards — useless even before this transition, and meaningless after it, since the colours they explained are gone. Replaced with single-select state filtering. Additive functionality rather than part of the transition proper, and should be scoped as such.
  • Counts are the point. Sixty-plus games a week and the problem is finding the two live ones. Counts on the chips answer "is anything live" without a click. Zero-count states render dimmed and unclickable so the absence is stated rather than implied.
  • Filter chips are neutral, not status-coloured — colouring them would reintroduce exactly what the transition removes, and spend palette on a persistent control. Active state is full-weight text and a brighter border.
  • Empty date groups hide under a filter rather than leaving orphaned headings. Implemented with :has(); production may prefer computing group visibility.
  • Matchups differs deliberately. An owner card holds roughly nine games, so scoreboards render expanded inline with no collapse — the volume does not justify hiding them, and the whole point of that view is reading a slate at once. Same component, different consumption. Odds show there for the same reason and are suppressed on Schedule's collapsed rows.
  • Preserved metadata: FCS marker (prefix slot), rank prefix, Top-25 / Ranked-spotlight eyebrow, neutral site, kickoff time, per-owner stat strip. The stat strip sits outside the scoreboard and is unchanged.
  • Sorted strictly by kickoff time, ascending, within each date group. A schedule's contract is time order, so live games are not floated to the top the way the Overview promotion model does — the filter covers "show me what's live" without breaking the one guarantee the view makes. Kickoff times therefore render on every row including finals: with the sort keyed to a value, hiding that value on two-thirds of the rows makes the order look arbitrary.
  • Open — landing position. Ascending order means a mid-Saturday visit opens on the morning's finals with live games below the fold. That is a scroll-position question rather than a sort question: options are to leave it, scroll to the first non-final game on load, or anchor the current date group. Worth deciding separately from the sort.
  • Two-column Schedule matches the Overview grid, row-major, container query at 760px. Matchups keeps its two-column owner-card grid.
  • Records shown throughout — anchor on scheduled rows, inline parenthetical on live and final rows, per the records follow-on. These depend on the CFBD records integration; until it lands, scheduled rows anchor on the spread and live/final rows omit the parenthetical.
  • FCS teams are never owned — they appear only as the unowned opponent in an FBS-vs-FCS game, so an FCS line renders team-only with no owner suffix, exactly like the unowned-FBS case. FCS-vs-FCS games cannot exist in the league and are not drawn.
  • The FCS marker is not redundant with "no owner". A 14-owner league holds fewer teams than the FBS contains, so unowned FBS opponents exist too — Purdue in the Schedule list is one. The marker distinguishes an FCS opponent from a merely-unowned FBS opponent, which matters because a win over each means something different. Both render without an owner suffix; only one carries the marker.
  • Defect — the sentinel NoClaim is rendered to members as an owner. The shipped Schedule shows "NoClaim vs LHooper" on the Massachusetts–Rutgers row. NoClaim is the league's internal convention for an unowned team, not a person; displayed as an owner name it reads as a fourteenth member. Rule: a NoClaim owner renders no owner suffix at all — the absence is the signal, exactly as it is for FCS opponents. Corrected throughout here (Massachusetts, Purdue).
  • The NoClaim bucket stays in the data model. It supports analysis and future insights over unowned teams, so this is a presentation rule only: never map the sentinel into the owner slot. The value is real; it just is not an owner.
  • Unowned FBS teams therefore exist — answering an earlier open question. Three unowned states now render distinctly: an FCS opponent (FCS marker, no owner), an unowned FBS team (no marker, no owner), and an owned team (owner suffix). Purdue and Massachusetts show the middle case; Abilene Christian and UAlbany the first.
  • Not addressed here: selection and ordering within either view, the Matrix sub-view, week-tab chrome, and the conference/team filters above the tabs.
